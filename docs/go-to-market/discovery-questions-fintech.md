# Discovery Questions: Fintech Compliance-Heavy Coordination Workflows

## Intro

Use these questions to validate whether fintech and compliance-heavy teams have a coordination-readiness problem that matches LS: fragmented review signals, slow go/no-go alignment, and a need for explainable, bounded advisory output.

## 1) Current workflow

- Walk me through your current release or go/no-go workflow from proposal to final approval.
- Which teams must align before a release can move forward?
- What information sources do each of those teams rely on today?
- Where do coordination discussions usually happen (meetings, docs, tickets, dashboards)?
- At what point does a decision become "ready" in your process?

## 2) Pain and failure modes

- Where do decisions slow down most often?
- What coordination failures are most expensive in your environment?
- What usually causes disagreement between product, compliance/risk, operations, and engineering/security?
- When decisions are delayed, what downstream impact do you see (timeline, rework, incident exposure)?
- Which part of the workflow feels most ambiguous under deadline pressure?

## 3) Explainability and review requirements

- What must be visible for a decision to feel review-ready?
- What do governance or compliance stakeholders need that dashboards or narrative summaries do not provide?
- How often do you need to justify or reconstruct why a release decision was made?
- What level of consistency is required across decisions for internal or external review?
- Which decision fields would you want to compare across releases?

## 4) Current tools and gaps

- What do your dashboards tell you well today?
- What do your current tools fail to tell you?
- Where do generic assistants help in this workflow today?
- Where do generic assistants fall short for high-stakes review?
- Which handoffs between tools or teams create the most interpretation loss?

## 5) Buying trigger and urgency

- What would need to happen for this problem to become urgent?
- Which is more costly for your organization right now: delayed releases or under-reviewed releases?
- If you had a compact, explainable readiness summary, who would care first?
- What recent incident or near-miss changed how leadership views release coordination risk?
- How do you prioritize this problem against other operational initiatives?

## 6) Adoption criteria

- What fields would need to exist in a decision-ready advisory object?
- What would make your team trust this kind of output?
- What would make your team reject this kind of output?
- Who would need to approve introducing an advisory layer into your workflow?
- What evidence would be required to move from pilot usage to regular usage?

## Desired outcomes from discovery

- Validate whether this coordination pain is persistent and costly.
- Confirm whether explainable coordination output matters in real review workflows.
- Test whether bounded advisory fields are more useful than free-form summaries.
- Decide whether fintech/compliance-heavy release coordination is a strong first wedge for LS.
