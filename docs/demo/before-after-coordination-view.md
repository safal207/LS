# Before/After Framing: Coordination Workflow vs LS Advisory Compression

## Purpose

This framing shows the contrast between a fragmented coordination workflow and a compact LS advisory view.

## Before: fragmented coordination reality

Teams typically operate across disconnected artifacts:

- operational dashboards,
- ticket queues,
- meeting notes,
- chat updates,
- cross-functional disagreements over evidence quality.

Result:

- no compact strategy-to-scene fit object,
- slower go/no-go discussions,
- higher ambiguity under time pressure.

## After: bounded LS advisory compression view

LS adds one bounded advisory interpretation object that surfaces:

- explicit coordination label,
- explicit readiness,
- explicit top risk driver,
- explicit intervention mode,
- explicit playbook support level.

Result:

- faster alignment discussions,
- consistent interpretation anchor across functions,
- easier traceability back to structured coordination layers.

## Suggested side-by-side visual

Use a simple 2-column slide/mock:

- **Left column (Before):** stacked icons/blocks for dashboards, tickets, notes, and disagreement markers.
- **Right column (After):** one central coordination advisory card plus 3 small traceability links (`snapshot`, `stabilization_order`, `playbook_advisory`).
- **Footer note:** "Advisory-only; human decision and governance remain in control."

Keep the layout minimal and readable from a distance (demo room / pitch deck context).

## Why this comparison works

- Makes the value visible in seconds without deep technical explanation.
- Connects directly to stakeholder pain (fragmentation, ambiguity, slow alignment).
- Shows that LS is a compression/interpretation layer, not an autonomous executor.
