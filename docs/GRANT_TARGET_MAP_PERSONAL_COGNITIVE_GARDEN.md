# Grant Target Map: Personal Cognitive Garden

## Purpose

This document maps the strongest near-term grant targets for the Personal Cognitive Garden direction.

Core thesis:

> LS turns AI sessions into governed, human-owned skill capital — with evidence, review, and anti-surveillance boundaries.

Use together with:

- `GRANT.md`
- `docs/GRANT_SUBMISSION_KIT_PERSONAL_COGNITIVE_GARDEN.md`
- `docs/GRANT_READY_BRIEF_PERSONAL_COGNITIVE_GARDEN.md`
- `docs/PERSONAL_COGNITIVE_GARDEN_RED_TEAM.md`
- `docs/PERSONAL_COGNITIVE_GARDEN_RUNNER.md`

## Priority ranking

```text
P0 — Schmidt Sciences / Trustworthy AI RFP, if active in current portal
P1 — NLnet NGI Zero Commons Fund
P1 — Long-Term Future Fund
P2 — Survival and Flourishing Fund
P2 — Emergent Ventures
P3 — Prototype Fund / German open-source programs
P3 — Future of Life Institute grants, if relevant call is open
```

## P0 — Schmidt Sciences / Trustworthy AI RFP

### Fit

Very strong if the active RFP is about trustworthy AI, scalable oversight, trace-based interpretability, evaluation, agentic behavior, or governance of AI systems.

### Best angle

```text
AI governance for personal memory and human development claims.
```

The project is strongest when framed as:

```text
A local-first, evidence-backed layer for evaluating AI-assisted human development without turning private growth data into surveillance.
```

### What to emphasize

- reviewable AI-assisted development claims;
- evidence-backed graph updates;
- local-first and human-owned state;
- anti-surveillance red-team scenario;
- schema + demo runner + reproducible artifact path;
- extension into an evaluation harness.

### What to avoid

- generic productivity tooling;
- HR analytics;
- employee scoring;
- self-help app framing;
- vague cognitive metaphor without artifact path.

### Suggested package

Use:

- 250-word abstract from submission kit;
- 6-month milestone plan;
- safety invariant;
- red-team scenario;
- demo runner link;
- schema/examples.

## P1 — NLnet NGI Zero Commons Fund

### Fit

Strong for open-source, commons-oriented, privacy-preserving infrastructure.

### Best angle

```text
Open-source, local-first infrastructure for human-owned AI development memory.
```

### What to emphasize

- public-interest infrastructure;
- local-first design;
- user autonomy;
- consent-safe data sharing;
- open schema;
- open-source reproducible demos;
- anti-surveillance boundary.

### Suggested package

Use:

- 100-word abstract;
- problem statement;
- proposed solution;
- 3-month milestone;
- budget framing for smaller grants;
- GRANT.md and live landing links.

### Suggested wording

```text
The project creates open-source infrastructure for helping people benefit from AI-assisted learning and work without surrendering private development data to employers or platforms.
```

## P1 — Long-Term Future Fund

### Fit

Strong if framed as AI safety / governance / oversight infrastructure for long-term AI adoption.

### Best angle

```text
Preventing unsafe personalization and surveillance incentives in AI-assisted work.
```

### What to emphasize

- AI systems increasingly infer human traits, goals, skills, and weaknesses;
- such inferences may become opaque profile state;
- personal development claims need evidence and consent;
- local-first review layer can reduce misuse;
- red-team scenario shows blocked employer access.

### Suggested package

Use:

- 250-word abstract;
- core research question;
- safety contribution;
- 6-month milestone;
- evaluation metrics;
- reviewer demo script.

### Suggested wording

```text
This project investigates how AI systems can support human development while preventing unauthorized profiling, private-graph extraction, and employer-facing surveillance.
```

## P2 — Survival and Flourishing Fund

### Fit

Potentially strong but broader and more competitive. Best if framed as infrastructure that improves governance, human agency, and safety under widespread AI adoption.

### Best angle

```text
Human agency and governance infrastructure for AI-mediated work and learning.
```

### What to emphasize

- preserving human agency as AI systems mediate more decisions;
- reviewable boundaries between assistance, memory, and action;
- anti-surveillance safeguards;
- open-source artifact with clear public benefit;
- future evaluation harness.

### Suggested package

Use:

- 100-word or 250-word abstract;
- problem statement;
- safety invariant;
- 6-month milestone;
- open artifact plan.

## P2 — Emergent Ventures

### Fit

Good for founder-led, fast-moving, unusual projects with a clear prototype and independent builder energy.

### Best angle

```text
A new category: human-owned skill-capital infrastructure for the AI agent era.
```

### What to emphasize

- unusual founder insight;
- working open-source prototype;
- fast iteration;
- grant-to-product path;
- paid pilot / money path;
- why now.

### Suggested package

Use:

- 25-word summary;
- 50-word summary;
- money path;
- launch sequence;
- demo runner;
- one strong personal founder paragraph.

### Suggested wording

```text
AI makes teams faster, but we do not know whether it makes people better. I am building LS to measure evidence-backed human development from AI sessions while keeping private growth graphs human-owned.
```

- public-interest digital infrastructure;

## P3 — Future of Life Institute grants

### Fit

Only apply if there is an active call matching AI safety, governance, or beneficial AI infrastructure.

### Best angle

```text
Governance layer for AI systems that infer and store human development claims.
```

### What to emphasize

- AI safety and governance;
- long-term risks from personalization and profiling;
- local-first alternative;
- open-source evaluation artifacts;
- red-team misuse scenarios.

## Recommended sequence

### Step 1 — Submit fastest open-source/public-interest application

Target:

```text
NLnet NGI Zero Commons Fund
```

Reason:

- strong open-source fit;
- local-first and privacy-preserving angle;
- clear artifact plan;
- smaller grant size fits schema/demo/evaluation work.

### Step 2 — Submit AI-safety/governance application

Target:

```text
Long-Term Future Fund
```

Reason:

- strong AI governance fit;
- red-team boundary is safety-relevant;
- project is early but artifact-backed.

### Step 3 — Submit founder-style fast grant

Target:

```text
Emergent Ventures
```

Reason:

- can sell the unusual insight and momentum;
- less formal than large research grants;
- good for quick independent-builder support.

### Step 4 — Larger / more competitive programs

Target:

```text
SFF
Schmidt Sciences / Trustworthy AI, if active
FLI, if matching call is open
```

Reason:

- stronger once the red-team runner and small evaluation harness exist;
- more convincing after one or two smaller grants or pilots.

## Immediate checklist

1. Confirm which programs are currently open.
2. For each open program, create one application draft.
3. Reuse the grant submission kit; do not write from scratch.
4. Attach official links:
   - project site;
   - `GRANT.md`;
   - grant-ready brief;
   - demo runner;
   - red-team scenario;
   - schema.
5. Keep the framing consistent:

```text
AI-assisted human development governance, not HR analytics.
```

```text
1. NLnet NGI Zero Commons Fund

3. Emergent Ventures
4. SFF
5. Schmidt / FLI / Prototype Fund depending on active calls and eligibility
```

## One-line close

> Start with the grants that value open artifacts, local-first governance, and safety boundaries. Use larger AI-safety grants once the evaluation harness and red-team runner are stronger.
