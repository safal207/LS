## 2. What are you building?

### Short answer

I am building LS, a local-first coordination and oversight runtime for
human-plus-model systems. It turns multi-model and multi-signal reasoning into
reviewable council cycles, replayable traces, operator approval workflows, and
machine-readable quality artifacts.

### Medium answer

I am building LS as infrastructure for supervising agentic systems. Instead of
treating model output as disposable text, LS records council cycles, tracks
which participants influenced the final outcome, measures whether the receiver
accepted the result cleanly, and exposes operator-facing approval and artifact
inspection flows. The repository also connects these cycles to contribution,
reputation, and merit signals, and supports replay and inspection through LTP.
On the evaluation side, it produces machine-readable quality reports and quality
gates that can be used in CI and local dashboards. The core idea is to make
agent coordination inspectable and governable rather than just capable.

### Technical one-liner

LS is an oversight substrate for agentic systems: council-ledger artifacts,
receiver resonance, contribution attribution, replayable traces, and
approval-safe operator workflows.

## 4. What is novel here?

### Short answer

The novelty is not just multi-model coordination. It is making that coordination
measurable, attributable, replayable, and operator-supervised.

### Medium answer

Many systems can orchestrate multiple models, but fewer systems preserve a
clear, inspectable record of who proposed what, what was adopted, whether the
receiver accepted the outcome, how an operator intervened, and how the path can
be replayed later. LS tries to connect those layers into one loop: council
artifacts, contribution and merit updates, receiver resonance, approval-safe
operator controls, and replayable traces. The novelty is in treating oversight
as a first-class runtime concern rather than an external reporting layer.
