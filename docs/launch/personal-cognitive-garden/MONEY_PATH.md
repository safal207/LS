# Money Path: Personal Cognitive Garden

## Core commercial thesis

> Companies are buying AI tools, but they cannot prove whether people are becoming more capable.

LS provides:

```text
AI adoption proof without employee surveillance.
```

In plain terms:

```text
Measure whether AI helps people improve — without exposing private growth data.
```

## What we sell first

Do not sell a platform first.

Sell a paid pilot.

The first paid offer is:

```text
2-week AI Skill-Growth Audit
```

The audit evaluates real AI sessions and returns:

```text
private reviewed graph per participant
aggregate team learning map
skill-delta report
anti-surveillance boundary report
recommendations for better AI adoption
```

## First buyer

Best first buyer:

```text
AI-heavy engineering or product team with 5-20 people actively using AI tools.
```

Why this buyer:

- already pays for AI tools;
- feels pressure to justify AI adoption;
- has observable artifacts: PRs, docs, issues, tests, design notes;
- understands evidence and review;
- does not need a full HR product;
- can start with a small pilot.

## Buyer pain

The buyer says:

```text
We pay for Cursor, ChatGPT, Claude, Codex, Copilot, or internal agents.
People use them every day.
Output increased.
But we do not know whether our people are actually getting better.
And we do not want a surveillance dashboard.
```

This is the opening wedge.

## Buyer promise

The promise:

```text
In two weeks, we will show which AI sessions created evidence-backed skill growth, which sessions were only activity, and what aggregate learning pattern appeared across the team — without exposing private individual growth graphs.
```

## SaaS later

Only after pilots show repeatable demand.

Possible later pricing:

```text
Individual Pro: $15-$30/month
Team: $10-$25/user/month
Org: custom pricing
Enterprise/Governance: annual contract
```

But early revenue should come from paid pilots and advisory, not self-serve SaaS.

## Why paid pilots first

Paid pilots are better than premature SaaS because:

- the market language is still being tested;
- the buyer is not yet educated;
- the product category is new;
- the safety boundary needs trust;
- each pilot creates examples, testimonials, and evidence;
- service revenue can fund productization.

## Investor narrative

```text
AI adoption is becoming a board-level spend, but measurement is shallow.
Companies can measure usage, tokens, velocity, and output.
They cannot measure whether AI is developing human capability.

At the same time, measuring people creates surveillance risk.
A system that exposes private goals, weak skills, reflections, and uncertainty will be rejected by workers and dangerous for employers.

LS solves this with a governed skill-capital layer:
AI sessions are classified by developmental effect, evidence-backed skill deltas are proposed, people review their private graph, and teams receive only aggregate-safe learning signals.

The first product is a paid AI Skill-Growth Audit for AI-heavy teams.
The long-term platform is human-owned skill-capital observability for the agent era.
```
