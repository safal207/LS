# Pilot Offer: Personal Cognitive Garden

## One-line offer

> AI adoption proof without employee surveillance.

## Simple promise

AI makes teams faster. This pilot tests whether AI also makes people better.

The Personal Cognitive Garden pilot turns real AI sessions into reviewed, evidence-backed skill-development signals while keeping each person's private growth graph under their control.

## Who this is for

Best early pilots:

- AI-heavy developer teams;
- QA teams using AI for test design, debugging, and analysis;
- founders using AI for product strategy and decision-making;
- research teams using AI for writing, synthesis, and ideation;
- AI adoption consultants who need proof of learning impact.

This is not an HR scoring product.

This is a safe way to understand whether AI-assisted work is creating real capability growth.

## Pilot format

```text
Duration: 2 weeks
Participants: 5-10 people
Input: 50-100 AI sessions
Output: private skill graphs + aggregate learning map + safety-boundary report
```

## What we collect

Only with participant consent:

- selected AI session summaries;
- participant-approved session notes;
- evidence artifacts such as PRs, test cases, docs, decisions, or learning outputs;
- participant review decisions: accepted, rejected, or needs revision;
- non-sensitive metadata about session type and developmental effect.

## What we do not collect

By default, the pilot must not collect or expose:

- private emotional state;
- raw private transcripts without consent;
- private weakness maps for managers;
- motivation history;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- rejected private reflections;
- individual performance scores;
- employer-facing private cognitive graphs.

## What each participant receives

Each participant receives a private reviewed development graph:

```text
goals
skills
decisions
constraints
evidence
practice loops
accepted growth updates
```

The participant can see:

- which AI sessions were developmental;
- what skill deltas were proposed;
- what evidence supports each claim;
- what was accepted or rejected;
- what practice loop should come next.

## What the team receives

The team receives only aggregate-safe output:

```text
team-level developmental session ratio
common skill areas
common practice loops
common blockers
safe recommendations for better AI adoption
```

The team does not receive private individual cognitive graphs.

## What the report includes

The final pilot report includes three parts.

### 1. Skill-delta report

Shows which session types most often created development signals.

Example:

```text
Architecture review sessions -> stronger boundary-setting skill
QA debugging sessions -> stronger failure-pattern recognition
Product strategy sessions -> stronger positioning clarity
```

### 2. Aggregate learning map

Shows safe team-level learning patterns without exposing private individual state.

Example:

```text
Most common development areas:
- test strategy
- product framing
- risk reasoning
- architecture tradeoff analysis
```

### 3. Safety-boundary report

Shows what remained private, what required consent, and what was blocked.

Example:

```text
Private graph export request -> BLOCK
Raw transcript sharing -> REQUIRES CONSENT
Aggregate skill trend -> ALLOW
Individual weakness ranking -> BLOCK
```

## Success criteria

The pilot is successful if it can show:

- at least 50 reviewed AI sessions;
- clear separation between developmental and non-developmental sessions;
- participant agreement or disagreement with proposed skill deltas;
- evidence-backed accepted updates;
- aggregate insights useful to the team;
- no exposure of private growth graphs;
- a practical path to repeated use.

## Buyer pain

The buyer pain is simple:

```text
We are paying for AI tools.
People are using them.
But we do not know whether people are becoming more capable.
And we do not want to turn AI adoption analytics into surveillance.
```
