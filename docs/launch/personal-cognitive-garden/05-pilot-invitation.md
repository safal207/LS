# Page 5 — The Invitation: Pilot, Grant, or Investor Conversation

## Big idea

The next step is not a massive platform. The next step is a small pilot.

The Personal Cognitive Garden can be tested with a narrow, practical question:

> Across real AI sessions, which interactions actually developed human capability?

## Pilot offer

A simple pilot can run for two weeks.

```text
Duration: 2 weeks
Users: 5-10 people
Input: 50-100 AI sessions
Output: skill-delta report + aggregate learning map + safety-boundary report
```

## Who the pilot is for

The first pilot is best for AI-heavy teams:

- developers using coding agents;
- QA engineers using AI for test design and debugging;
- founders using AI for product strategy;
- researchers using AI for ideation and writing;
- teams experimenting with agentic workflows.

## What the pilot measures

The pilot should not measure people as employees.

It should measure AI sessions as development events.

Example questions:

```text
Which sessions were developmental?
Which skills changed?
What evidence supports the claim?
Which updates were accepted by the person?
Which sessions were useful but non-developmental?
Which agent inferences were rejected?
Which signals can be shared safely in aggregate?
```

## Pilot outputs

The pilot can produce:

1. **Individual private graph**
   - owned by the participant;
   - not exposed to the employer by default;
   - includes accepted goals, skills, decisions, evidence, reflections, and growth paths.

2. **Aggregate learning map**
   - team-level developmental session ratio;
   - common skill areas;
   - common practice loops;
   - no private reflections;
   - no individual weakness ranking.

3. **Safety-boundary report**
   - what was blocked;
   - what required human review;
   - what was safe to share;
   - what remained private.

## For grant reviewers

The grant path asks:

> How can AI systems measure human development without creating unauthorized profiling or surveillance?

A grant can fund:

- schema hardening;
- red-team runner expansion;
- evaluation examples;
- pilot study design;
- open technical note;
- consent and export policy.

## For investors

The investor path asks:

> AI adoption is expensive. Who proves whether people are actually getting better?

The commercial wedge is:

```text
AI-session review layer for developer and knowledge-worker growth.
```

The first buyer is not the entire HR market.

The first buyer is an AI-heavy team that wants to understand whether AI adoption is creating capability, not just output.

## Simple CTA

For a pilot conversation:

```text
We are testing a 2-week Personal Cognitive Garden pilot with 5-10 AI-heavy users.
The goal is to classify 50-100 AI sessions, identify skill deltas, produce private reviewed graphs, and return an aggregate-safe learning map.
```

For a grant conversation:

```text
We are building a governance layer for AI-assisted human development claims: schema, demo runner, red-team scenarios, evaluation cases, and consent-safe export rules.
```

For an investor conversation:

```text
Companies are adopting AI agents, but they cannot tell whether people are becoming more capable. LS shows whether AI sessions compound into human-owned skill capital without turning growth into surveillance.
```

## Final line

> AI makes teams faster. LS shows whether it makes people better.
